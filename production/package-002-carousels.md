# Package 002 — Carousel copy

## Carousel A — Four ways an outfit can feel like armor

1. **Cover:** Why does the right outfit feel like armor?
2. **The body:** Pressure, temperature, texture, weight and movement change the body’s sensory conditions.
3. **The meaning:** The outfit can cue a role, memory or intention. This is interpretation—not fabric magic.
4. **The room:** Other people read dress and may change their behavior. Their response can change yours.
5. **The ritual:** Repeating an outfit before difficult work can mark the transition from preparation to action.
6. **The cost:** Protection can coexist with pain, masking, restriction or unwanted visibility.
7. **The boundary:** “Armor” names a combined experience. Science has not found one universal confidence switch.
8. **Action:** Map your pathway in the full AAN × Fashion exhibition.

## Carousel B — Claim / evidence / boundary

1. **Cover:** Your outfit feels powerful. What does that prove?
2. **Claim:** “My clothes changed my nervous system.”
3. **Evidence:** Clothing does change ongoing tactile, thermal and movement-related input.
4. **Boundary:** That does not identify a confidence circuit or show reduced threat.
5. **Claim:** “People respected me more.”
6. **Evidence:** Dress contributes to person perception and social inference.
7. **Boundary:** Social judgments can be biased; the garment may have changed the room, not competence.
8. **Claim:** “I always perform better in this outfit.”
9. **Evidence:** A repeated preparation ritual can be a plausible personal pathway.
10. **Boundary:** Compare several occasions. One memory is not a causal experiment.
