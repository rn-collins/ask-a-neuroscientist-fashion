# Carousel 1 — The study everyone quotes
1. Can clothes change cognition?
2. The 2012 lab-coat study reported fewer errors on incongruent Stroop trials.
3. Its theory required symbolic meaning + physically wearing the garment.
4. That is narrower than “dress for success.”
5. The task was attention—not IQ, creativity or brain rewiring.
6. Then came a preregistered direct replication.
7. Continue: The replication people omit.

# Carousel 2 — The replication people omit
1. The famous effect did not reproduce.
2. Burns, Fox, Greenstein, Olbricht and Montgomery repeated the critical design under preregistration.
3. The lab-coat Stroop effect was not recovered.
4. Null does not mean clothing never matters.
5. It means the viral sentence does not fit the evidence.
6. Best label: disputed / qualified.
7. Read the source and boundary in the Evidence Room. DOI: 10.1016/j.jesp.2018.10.001.

## Design and accessibility direction

- 1080 × 1350 source master; keep all text inside a 96 px safe boundary.
- Use the Met Public Domain corset only on Carousel 1 and the costume armor only on Carousel 2; never use paper figures without permission.
- Place the evidence label in words at the top of every slide: REPORTED, MEASURED, NOT MEASURED, REPLICATED, or BOUNDARY.
- Minimum 48 px body text; one sentence per slide; high-contrast bone on ink.
- Alt text must reproduce the complete slide statement and identify the object when shown.
