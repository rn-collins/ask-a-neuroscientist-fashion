## Carousel A — Signal is not proof

1. **A uniform is read before its wearer speaks.** / Met 1979.152.54a–h, Public Domain/CC0.
2. **VISIBLE:** color, cut, insignia, equipment, repetition.
3. **INFERRED:** role, status, competence, safety, threat, belonging.
4. **KNOWN:** verified credential, conduct, policy, relationship, institutional record.
5. **HISTORY:** personal encounters, media, community memory, culture.
6. **THE GAP MATTERS.** Inference is not knowledge of a person.
7. **MAP ONE ENCOUNTER.** Uniform Encounter Map · no storage · no score.

Caption: Person-perception evidence shows that dress contributes to social inference. It does not turn appearance into truth. Source: Hester & Hehman (2023), DOI 10.1177/10888683231157961.

## Carousel B — How strong is the evidence?

1. **DO UNIFORMS CHANGE BEHAVIOR?** The honest answer needs a fitting.
2. **REVIEW:** Dress contributes to person perception. Strong enough to discuss signaling; not mind-reading.
3. **EXPERIMENT:** Students in police-style uniforms showed one status-attention bias. Not police conduct.
4. **EXPERIMENT:** Military/Red Cross clothing shifted one moral-choice task. Not real-world service.
5. **fMRI:** Uniform self-images engaged distributed networks. No “uniform center.”
6. **WORKPLACE CASE:** Uniform change rearranged hospital professional boundaries. One institution, not all.
7. **FITTED CLAIM:** Uniforms can organize perception and make roles salient. They do not contain a fixed psychology.
