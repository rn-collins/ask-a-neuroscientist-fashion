# AAN-F-007 — Carousel masters

## Carousel A — Signal is not proof

1. **A uniform is read before its wearer speaks.**
2. **What is visible:** color, cut, insignia, equipment, repetition.
3. **What gets inferred:** role, status, competence, safety, threat, belonging.
4. **What is actually known:** verified credential, conduct, policy, relationship, institutional record.
5. **What arrives with us:** personal encounters, media, community memory and culture.
6. **The gap matters.** Inference is not knowledge of a person.
7. **Before one signal becomes a verdict, map one encounter.** The uniform may be immediately legible. The person inside it is not.

Caption: Person-perception evidence shows that dress contributes to social inference. It does not turn appearance into truth. Source: Hester & Hehman (2023), DOI 10.1177/10888683231157961.

## Carousel B — How strong is the evidence?

1. **Do uniforms change behavior?** The honest answer needs a fitting.
2. **Clothing shapes first impressions.** That is not mind-reading.
3. **In one study, students wearing police-style uniforms showed a status-attention bias.** It did not study police conduct.
4. **Military and Red Cross clothing shifted one moral-choice task.** It did not measure real-world service.
5. **Seeing yourself in uniform engages distributed networks.** There is no “uniform center.”
6. **In one hospital, changing uniforms rearranged professional boundaries.** One institution is not every workplace.
7. **The honest conclusion:** uniforms can organize perception and make roles salient. They do not contain a fixed psychology.
