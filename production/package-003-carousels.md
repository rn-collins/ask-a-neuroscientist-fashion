## Carousel A · “Soft” is not a material property (10 slides)
1. WHY CAN ONE FABRIC FEEL CALMING AND ANOTHER UNBEARABLE?
2. Start here: your skin does not read the fiber label.
3. It receives pressure · vibration · stretch · temperature · friction · chemical signals.
4. Aβ afferents help discriminate contact. C-tactile afferents contribute to affective gentle touch. Itch and pain add other messages.
5. Fiber becomes yarn. Yarn becomes weave or knit. Construction adds seams, labels, closures, tension and layers.
6. A 2024 study: tactile sensitivity changed textile ratings. Boundary: hand-touch lab task ≠ hours of wear.
7. Heat, moisture, movement, duration, body site and expectation can change the same garment.
8. “Natural,” “soft” and “sensory-friendly” are not standardized comfort guarantees.
9. Evidence-fitted answer: comfort is an encounter among textile, garment, body and context.

## Carousel B · Map a garment without diagnosing yourself (8 slides)
1. THIS GARMENT. THIS BODY. THIS DAY.
2. Name the exact garment—not just “wool” or “cotton.”
3. Locate the contact: neck, waist, forearm, foot, whole body?
4. Observe: surface · pressure · heat/moisture · movement/repetition.
5. Add context: quiet, crowded, working, exercising, after long wear.
6. Choose words, not verdicts: settling, neutral, distracting, itchy, painful, entrapping.
7. Test one change. Do not force exposure to harm.
8. This is an observation tool—not an autism, ADHD, allergy or pain screen. [Tool URL]
