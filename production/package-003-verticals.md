# Package 003 — seven vertical scripts

## 1 · Your skin cannot read a fiber label (35s)
**Spoken:** “Cotton” cannot tell your nervous system what comes next. Yarn, weave, finish, seam, fit, heat, moisture and movement all change the contact. A fiber label names ingredients. Comfort is an encounter.  
**Picture:** macro textile → label → inside seam → moving sleeve.  
**On screen:** FIBER ≠ FABRIC ≠ GARMENT ≠ EXPERIENCE.  
**Caption:** Full evidence and public-domain objects at the exhibition. No universal comfort claims.

## 2 · Soft is relational (40s)
**Spoken:** A 2024 study found that measured tactile sensitivity changed how participants rated textile textures. But they touched samples by hand in a lab. They did not wear a seam for eight hours. The evidence says people differ—not that a laboratory swatch predicts your day.  
**Picture:** two hands; then garment at neck and waist.  
**On screen:** SAME TEXTILE · DIFFERENT NERVOUS SYSTEM · DIFFERENT CONTEXT.

## 3 · The label problem (30s)
**Spoken:** If one tiny label moves against your skin twice a second, that is 7,200 contacts in an hour. The problem may not be the shirt. It may be repetition at one body site.  
**Picture:** macro label motion; timer.  
**Boundary:** Illustrative arithmetic, not a measured universal contact rate.

## 4 · There is no autism fabric list (45s)
**Spoken:** Ten autistic adults in a mixed-methods study described intense clothing distress—and positive sensory seeking. That is evidence for taking experience seriously. It is not permission to say every autistic person hates wool, loves cotton or needs compression. The finding is variability.  
**Picture:** distinct garments; no stock portrait standing in for autism.  
**On screen:** TESTIMONY ≠ STEREOTYPE.

## 5 · Calming is not sewn into compression (35s)
**Spoken:** Steady pressure may feel organizing to one person and entrapping to another. Small studies of sensory and compression garments do not prove a universal calming treatment. Preference matters. Marketing is not mechanism.  
**Picture:** elastic stretched at different tensions.  
**On screen:** INDIVIDUAL RESPONSE ≠ THERAPEUTIC GUARANTEE.

## 6 · When clothes hurt (35s)
**Spoken:** In tactile allodynia, normally innocuous contact—sometimes even a garment brushing skin—can produce pain. That is not the same as every sensory aversion. New or persistent burning, numbness, rash or pain deserves clinical assessment.  
**Picture:** fabric stops before touching skin; source title.  
**On screen:** EDUCATION · NOT DIAGNOSIS.

## 7 · One-variable garment test (45s)
**Spoken:** Name the exact garment. Name the body site. Then track surface, pressure, heat, moisture, movement, duration and context. Change one thing: inside-out, base layer, looser fit, cooler room. You are not scoring your nervous system. You are learning what this encounter asks of it.  
**Picture:** screen capture of Field Map.  
**CTA:** Use the private, no-upload tool in the gallery.
