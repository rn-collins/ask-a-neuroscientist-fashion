# AAN-F-007 — Seven vertical scripts

Each: 35–55 seconds. Burned captions plus clean caption file. Close on `SIGNAL ≠ PROOF` and link the full exhibition.

## 01 — Before they speak
**Hook:** “A uniform is read before its wearer speaks.”

**Script:** Dress joins face, body and context in person perception. A uniform can trigger fast guesses about role, status, competence or authority. But a guess is not a credential, and it is not access to character. The meaning changes with the observer, institution, setting and history. Signal is not proof.

**Visual:** Detail crops of Met 1979.152.54a–h with full credit. **Boundary:** Review evidence; no universal effect.

## 02 — The police-style uniform study
**Hook:** “This study did not test police officers.”

**Script:** Canadian students wore a police-style uniform in reaction-time experiments. Attention shifted toward people pictured in hoodies, used as a low-status cue. The study did not find the predicted race bias. This is narrow evidence about students, pictures and response time—not proof about policing behavior.

**Visual:** Typography only; do not stage or costume policing. **Source:** Civile & Obhi, 2017.

## 03 — No uniform center
**Hook:** “A brain scan found no magic uniform switch.”

**Script:** In a small fMRI study, people judged adjectives while seeing themselves or others in professional uniform and casual clothes. Distributed self, social and affective systems were involved. That pattern is task-specific. It does not show a uniform center or prove cloth manufactured professional pride.

**Source:** Hong et al., 2019.

## 04 — The institution travels
**Hook:** “The badge is not powerful by itself.”

**Script:** Law, training, hierarchy, recognition and lived history give a uniform institutional force. One hospital study found that changing staff uniforms altered professional boundaries and status. Design did not merely change the look; it rearranged who was legible as what.

**Source:** Timmons & East, 2011.

## 05 — Belonging is not guaranteed
**Hook:** “Matching clothes can gather a group—and sharpen its edge.”

**Script:** A uniform can make membership visible and a shared role salient. It can also erase difference, enforce conformity or mark outsiders. No garment guarantees belonging. Ask who chose it, who can alter it, and who pays the cost of not fitting.

**Boundary:** Synthesis, not a universal causal claim.

## 06 — Fashion’s uniform brief
**Hook:** “A beautiful uniform can fail its wearer.”

**Script:** The real design brief includes movement, heat, sensory load, religious practice, gender expression, varied bodies, laundering, replacement, safety and refusal. If the image works but the required wearer cannot work, the design is not finished.

**Visual:** Met objects plus written checklist; never imply the historic objects meet modern standards.

## 07 — Four columns
**Hook:** “Try this before deciding what a uniform means.”

**Script:** Column one: what was actually visible. Two: what you inferred. Three: what you independently knew. Four: which history or prior encounter shaped the reading. The gap between those columns is where responsible perception begins.
