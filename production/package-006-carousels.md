# AAN-F-006 — Two finished carousels

## Carousel A — Anatomy of a runway soundtrack
1. **The clothes arrive inside a sound world.**
2. **Scene:** the ear groups music, steps, voices and room noise.
3. **Pulse:** a beat offers a forecast for the next moment.
4. **Expectation:** repetition and surprise shape attention.
5. **Movement:** rhythm may invite alignment; it does not command it.
6. **Image + memory:** sound frames what you see and may cue your history.
7. **The fitted claim:** sound can organize a runway without controlling its people. Sources and Sound Map in the gallery.

Design: black field, white type, one cobalt timing line. Use the Met CC0 metronome, drum and music-box photographs on slides 3–5 with full object credit. No commercial audio.

## Carousel B — Entrainment does not mean hypnosis
1. **No: a runway beat does not take over a brain.**
2. In music science, entrainment describes alignment with temporal structure.
3. Beat perception involves distributed auditory and motor systems.
4. People may tap, sway, predict—or choose not to move.
5. Models also negotiate shoes, garments, choreography, floor and rehearsal.
6. Lab tapping and clinical gait studies are adjacent evidence, not proof of a Fashion Week mechanism.
7. **Use the right verb: invites, supports, cues. Not controls.**

Footer: Patel & Iversen 2014; Merchant et al. 2015; Schaffert et al. 2019. Full links in gallery.
